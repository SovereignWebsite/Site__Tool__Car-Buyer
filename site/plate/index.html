What a plate tells you

Many facts about a car are public. Who owns it is not.

Kind of checkExampleWhat it means for you
Free official websiteDVLA vehicle enquiry, DVSA MOT historyAnyone can look. Make, fuel, engine, dates, colour, tax and MOT status; MOT results, mileage and advisories.
Official API, with a keyDVLA Vehicle Enquiry Service, DVSA MOT history APIFor services that register and accept the terms. Not something a static site can call for you.
Paid history reportFinance, theft and write-off checksFrom commercial providers. Coverage depends on the provider.
RestrictedThe registered keeper's name and address, insurance policy detailsNot public. Keeper details need a DVLA request with reasonable cause (form V888).

Sources: GOV.UK, vehicle information from DVLA; GOV.UK, MOT history; DVLA, V888.

The age identifier

Since September 2001 a registration has two letters for the area, then two numbers that tell you when it was issued, then three random letters. The numbers change twice a year: in March they are the year (26 for March to August 2026), and in September the year plus 50 (76 for September 2026 to February 2027). A registration cannot be used to make a car look younger than it is, but a private number can make it look older, so check the first registration date on the V5C and with DVLA. Type a registration on Check a car to see its period. (DVLA, INF104, pages 5 and 6.)

The papers, and what they do not prove

ItemWhat it isWhat it does not prove
Registration numberThe number on the platesThat the seller is honest: plates can be cloned or moved
VINThe vehicle identification number on the carThat the seller owns it: compare it with the papers
V5C (logbook)DVLA's record of the registered keeperOwnership: the keeper and the owner can differ
Green new keeper slip (V5C/2)The part the seller gives you, with a reference you can use to tax the carInsurance, a guarantee, or a reason not to see the full V5C
MOTA test of set safety and environmental itemsThe general condition of the engine, clutch or gearbox
Vehicle taxA national tax on the vehicleInsurance, an MOT, or freedom from London charges

Do not post a V5C, keeper slip, insurance certificate or driving licence online without hiding its references. The Information Commissioner says a number plate is personal data in most circumstances where it can identify someone (ICO).
